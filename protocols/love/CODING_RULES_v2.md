## Question M — What does love DO in this work? (choose exactly one)
Read the painting AND the statement; for this question the statement is the main key to the metaphor.
1. `union` — two become one: merging, fusing, two halves completing one heart, braided into a single strand. (Plato's Aristophanes; the "union view".)
2. `together_apart` — two stay distinct but bound: side by side, leaning, sharing roots or ground, keeping a space between them. "Two bodies, one heart." (The "federation" view.)
3. `third_born` — something new is born between them: a third color, a third light, a flame, a seedling, a child of light that belongs to neither alone.
4. `shelter` — love protects something fragile: arms, arcs, a nest, a roof around a small light or a young one. (The "robust concern" view.)
5. `waiting_return` — love waits and welcomes: a lamp left on, a path home, someone not yet arrived.
6. `illuminating` — love is attention that makes others visible or lit.
7. `mending` — love repairs: cracks filled with gold, stitching, healing a break.
8. `longing` — reaching across a distance not yet closed; seeking, yearning, almost touching.
9. `gathering` — many separate things converge into one shared warmth.
10. `other` — none of the above; describe it in the note.
Tie-break: if the statement explicitly says a new thing arises between them → `third_born`; if it stresses each remaining itself → `together_apart`; if it stresses becoming one → `union`.

## Question N — How many beings/entities carry the love? (choose one)
`one` · `two` · `many` (three or more) · `none` (no distinct entities at all, e.g. a pure field of color).
Count what the work presents as the lovers/carriers (two lights = two; two trees = two; a lamp and an absent walker = two; a nest of parents and chick = many).
